# Itinerary Rules

- Treat arrival and departure days as constrained days. Add airport/station, baggage, check-in, and transfer buffers before sightseeing.
- Schedule must-go places first, then fill remaining time with interest-aligned stops. Keep the requested pace: light, standard, or compact.
- Do not put two distant stops back-to-back without a stated transfer or buffer.
- Give each tight or time-sensitive stop a `deadline`, and give each fragile stop a `fallback` or skip note.
- Keep opening hours, prices, and transport schedules as uncertain unless the user supplied current evidence or an official source was readable.
- When a day becomes impossible, preserve the user's must-go place and remove optional stops before compressing buffers.
- Avoid inventing restaurants, tickets, reservations, or route durations. Use a generic stop description and a warning when evidence is thin.
- Keep the site execution-focused: next stop, map action, timing, buffer, and fallback should be visible without returning to source notes.
